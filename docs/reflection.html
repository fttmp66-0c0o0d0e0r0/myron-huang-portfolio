1. How I worked

I built the site with Google Antigravity as a coding assistant. I started by telling it which sections the assignment needed, then went back and forth on the layout, the images and the wording until the page looked like me. Most of my time went into small fixes after the first draft, not the draft itself. Once it looked right on my own computer, I prepared it for GitHub Pages.

2. What I changed by hand

The first version came out dark, with neon colors that felt like a gaming site. I now work on medical imaging and used to work at HP and Quanta, so I asked for a light baby-blue and white style instead.

The AI also drew my MayaMiner figurine as a doctor in a white coat with a stethoscope, because the scene was an operating room. I wrote the software that ran in that room. I never operated on anyone. It took several tries before the figure wore glasses and a shirt and held a laptop.

The text was too long as well. I cut it down to short labels and let the pictures explain each job. I also moved sections around, deleted the long About Me paragraphs and the skill bars, fixed job titles and dates (Quanta 2019-2023, HP 2023-2025), and renamed the main section Education / Experience. The AI could not have known any of these details unless I told it.

3. What I learned

A generated page looks finished very quickly, and that makes it easy to stop checking. I still had to read the code, make sure the image paths would work on GitHub Pages, and open the page in the browser after every change.

The tool saved me a lot of time on layout and on making the pictures. But facts about my own career had to come from me. If I did this again, I would write the content first and only then ask the AI to lay it out.
