1. How I worked

I built the site with Google Antigravity as a coding assistant. I started by giving it my exported LinkedIn PDF and my past CV, and told it which sections the assignment needed: mainly my experience, skills and current research, without too much about hobbies or private life that have little to do with my work. Antigravity then set up localhost:8038 so I could preview the template. To adjust the layout, I used the Snipping Tool on my laptop to circle the block I wanted changed, then attached the screenshot or text to my prompt. I went back and forth on the layout, the images and the wording until the page matched my own style. Most of my time went into small fixes after the first draft, not the draft itself. Once the site looked fine on my own computer, I deployed it to GitHub Pages.

2. What I changed by hand

The first version came out dark, with neon colors that felt like a gaming site. Since I now work in medical imaging and did not want the page to feel too techy, I asked for a light baby-blue and white style to make it feel gentle and approachable.

Antigravity also drew the figurine in my MayaMiner picture as a doctor in a white coat with a stethoscope, because the scene was an operating room. In fact I wrote software that ran other machines in that room, and I never operated on anyone. After several tries, the figure ended up with glasses, a shirt and a laptop.

The text was also too long. Whatever material I gave the AI, it filled the page with all of it. I cut it down to a few short labels and let the pictures show what each job was. I also reordered the sections, deleted the long About Me paragraphs and the skill bars, fixed job titles and dates (Quanta 2019-2023, HP 2023-2025), and renamed the main section Education / Experience. The AI could not have known these details unless I gave them to it. When the work was about 70% done, I told Antigravity not to add features on its own or search the web for extra information unless I approved it with a new instruction.

3. What I learned

A generated page looks complete very quickly, which makes it easy to relax and stop checking. I still had to read the code myself, confirm that the image paths would work on GitHub Pages, and reopen the page in the browser after every change.

The tool saved me a lot of time on layout and image generation, but the facts about my own career still had to come from me.
